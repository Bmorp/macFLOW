# Mac Flow — "Look up" — review notes

46.5 s, 1920 × 1080, 30 fps. Output: `renders/mac-flow-look-up.mp4`.

## Creative calls

- Built storyline **A, "Look up"** (`STORYLINES.md`): the camera lives at the notch of a MacBook; the notch wakes with a song,
  the island becomes each thing a real morning needs (Join a meeting, ask Flow for focus + music → the island splits,
  a build races to done, AirPods, drop a file), a 2× montage of every presentation, Home opens as the camera pulls back
  to the whole Mac, "Everything live. Just look up.", and the notch drips off the top edge into the Mac Flow mark.
  Not built: **B** "One afternoon, one Mac" (clock-driven day), **C** "Doing nothing" (kinetic type + tint floods).
- **Every island frame is the real app.** `tools/harness/FilmHarness.swift` drives the app's own SwiftUI views
  (`IslandSurface`, `IslandPresentation`, `LiquidSplit`) with the app's springs and demo data, at 3× on transparency.
  The 544 frames in `assets/frames` were rendered from a scratch copy of the app with `tools/harness/film-hooks.patch`
  applied (a `--film` flag, a frozen focus clock, and guards that keep real now-playing data, Spotlight files and
  keyboard focus out of the renders). The app's source in this repo was not modified.
- Demo content: the app's own demo track name ("Midnight City — M83") with an original cover (`tools/harness/artwork.png`),
  a FaceTime "Design Review", "Building Mac Flow", generic Claude session names, AirPods 92/88/64, "Q4 Proposal.pdf".
  The Dock and menu bar use this Mac's real app icons and SF Symbols; the wallpaper is original (`tools/wallpaper.py`).
- Copy is the README's where it states a fact ("A Dynamic Island for your Mac's notch", "On-device. Private.",
  "macOS 14 or later"). End card uses the brand kit's stacked logo paths unchanged.
- Sound: an original 120 BPM score written to the cut (`tools/score.py`), plus bundled Pixabay-licensed effects
  (`assets/audio/sfx/CREDITS.md`) placed by their measured peaks.

## Measured vs. looked at

- `npx hyperframes check`: passes, 0 errors.
- Looked at ~80 stills across every beat and seam (wake, reveal, click, whip, split, flood, drop, montage, Home, end card)
  and the phone-width sheet.
- Score measured, **not heard**: −15.0 LUFS integrated, −3.4 dBTP; section loudness builds intro −19.6 → groove −15
  → Flow breakdown −24 → montage/Home −12 → claim −22 → final hit −12.4 (short-term). The rendered MP4 with effects:
  −14.9 LUFS integrated, −1.1 dBTP true peak, loudest moment at 42.06 s (the star cut). Nobody has listened to it yet.
- Master: H.264 CRF 12, 1920×1080, 30 fps, AAC 48 kHz stereo, 22.8 MB; encoded frames checked for blocking in the dark glows.

## Weakest shot / with more time

- 11.85–12.35: the black title world contracts back as a plain rounded box; a real island-shaped contraction
  (shoulders included) would match the opening.
- The montage at 2× reads as energy rather than as individual states — intentional, but a 1.5× cut would show more.
- The score is synthesised; a licensed track (or HeyGen's music catalog after `heygen auth login`) would raise the ceiling.
  Swap `assets/audio/score.wav` (keep 46.5 s, hits at 6.0, 14.0, 30.0, 33.0, 42.0).

## Warnings kept on purpose

- `text_below_floor` / `line_unread` on the menu bar ("Finder File Edit…", the clock): it is the Mac's own chrome, not copy.
- `composition_file_too_large` on `compositions/stage.html`: it is generated by `tools/build_film.py`; edit the generator.
- Fonts: titles and the menu bar use the system SF Pro file copied into `assets/fonts/SFNS.ttf`. Check Apple's SF Pro
  licence for marketing use (the brand README already flags this for the wordmark); the `font-display` variable can swap it.

## Rebuild

```bash
python3 tools/score.py        # assets/audio/score.wav
python3 tools/build_film.py   # compositions/stage.html, compositions/titles.html, index.html
npx hyperframes render --quality high -o renders/mac-flow-look-up.mp4
```

Rendering needs a working `ffmpeg`. On this Mac Homebrew's ffmpeg 8.1.1 fails to load (`libx265.216.dylib` missing after
an x265 upgrade); `brew reinstall ffmpeg` fixes it. This render used a self-contained ffmpeg from the npm `ffmpeg-static` package.
